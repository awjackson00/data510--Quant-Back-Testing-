Portfolio Resilience: Backtesting Asset Allocation Strategies During Financial Crises

Dane Herrin & Alexander Jackson · Studio Session 4 · Peer Stakeholder POs: Ben Dyer, Luca Casano, Manish Reddy Kallu

Total return alone hides the risk it took to get there. We backtested 5 portfolio constructions — Barbell, Mega-Cap Technology, Energy, Real Estate, and FMCG — against 9 historical market regimes from the Dot-com crash through the post-2023 period, scoring each on Sharpe ratio, Sortino ratio, annualized return, and cumulative return rather than return alone. No single portfolio wins every regime, and that gap is the finding.

02What was tested

Defensive

Barbell

95% IEF (intermediate Treasuries) / 5% ^VIX. A theoretical volatility-sensitive comparison — VIX is not directly investable.

Growth

Mega-Cap Technology

Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Amazon, Google, Meta, Broadcom, Tesla. Dynamically equal-weighted across whichever names have traded on a given date.

Cyclical

Energy

10 holdings across oil & gas and utilities (Exxon, Chevron, ConocoPhillips, EOG, Schlumberger, NextEra, Duke, Constellation, Southern, Dominion), equal-weighted.

Rate-sensitive

Real Estate

100% Vanguard Real Estate ETF (VNQ). No Dot-com-era result — VNQ didn't begin trading until 2004.

Defensive

FMCG

Walmart, Costco, Target, P&G, Unilever, Kimberly-Clark, Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Nestlé, General Mills. Equal-weighted at 10% each.

Context

Benchmarks

SPY and market return minus the risk-free rate (Mkt−RF) provide broad-market context alongside the five constructed portfolios.

03Reproducibility

Full setup guide →

Clone & install

Python, NumPy, pandas, yfinance, Tidy Finance, matplotlib, seaborn, statsmodels, and scikit-learn. All price data is pulled fresh from Yahoo Finance via yfinance, so no raw market data is redistributed in the repo.

Rebuild the results

Run the notebooks in order: EDA → portfolio construction → SharpeRatioSheet.ipynb (the source of Table 1) → ARIMA modeling. Regime dates and portfolio weights are documented in the write-up so a peer can reproduce the exact analysis window.

04Ethics & data notice

Not investment advice. Every result in this project is historical and educational. Past portfolio performance does not demonstrate that any strategy will perform similarly in future markets.

Data & license

All data are public financial-market data sourced from Yahoo Finance via the open-source yfinance package, used here for research and educational purposes. The project contains no personally identifiable information, private investor data, or restricted financial records. Site and analysis code are shared under the MIT License; see the repository LICENSE file.

Backtesting carries known limitations we address directly in the write-up: hindsight bias (portfolios are evaluated after the crises are known), survivorship bias (holdings are today's recognizable companies), staggered asset inception dates (VNQ, IEF), the VIX not being directly investable, and the exclusion of transaction costs, taxes, and rebalancing costs. See the Discussion, Limitations, and Future Work section for the complete list.
